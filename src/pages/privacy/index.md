This policy explains what information Love Wins Media, Inc. ("we", "us") collects when you use AudienceKit and the community apps built on it, and what we do with it. It covers our websites, the AudienceKit admin console, and every audience app we run on the platform, including **Hot Mess** (hotmess.social) on iPhone, Android, the web and Alexa, and **Hack Different** (hackdiffe.rent), the community app for hackerspaces, security meetups and hacker conferences, on iPhone and Android. In this policy, "the apps" means all of these.

If you have a question, or want to see, correct or delete your information, email us at [rick@lovewins.media](mailto:rick@lovewins.media).

## What we collect

### When you sign in with Facebook

Hot Mess and AudienceKit offer Facebook Login. Hack Different doesn't: you sign in to it with Apple. When you sign in, Facebook shares the information you agree to on its permission screen. Depending on what you allow, that can include:

- your name, first and last name, profile picture and email address;
- an app-scoped Facebook ID, an ID that is the same across our own Facebook apps (so you keep one account if you sign in through more than one of them), and a sign-in token, so we can keep you signed in;
- in Hot Mess, your Facebook friend list (your contacts there), which we use only to find which of your friends also use Hot Mess, so you can see friends at the same venue;
- on AudienceKit, the list of Facebook Pages you manage, if you are a venue or organizer setting up an audience, so you can choose which Page to link.

We never post to Facebook for you, and we never see your Facebook password.

### When you sign in with Apple

You can sign in with Apple instead; in Hack Different it is the only way to sign in. Apple shares a stable ID for you, and the name and email address you choose to share the first time you sign in; if you choose Hide My Email, we get an address that forwards to you. So that deleting your account also ends the app's access to your Apple ID, we keep a sign-in token from Apple, encrypted, and revoke it with Apple when you delete your account. We never see your Apple ID password. You can connect Facebook later to see your friends in Hot Mess.

### When you sign in with Google

Hot Mess on Android and the AudienceKit admin console also offer Google sign-in. Google shares a stable ID for you and your email address, and your name if you allow it. We never see your Google password, and we don't read your Gmail, contacts, calendar or anything else in your Google account.

### When you connect Discord

Hack Different lets you connect your Discord account, if you choose to. Discord then shares your Discord user ID, username, display name and email address (and whether Discord has verified it). We use them to link your Discord name to your account and to find an account you already have. We never see your Discord password, we don't read your Discord messages, servers or friends through this connection, and we never post to Discord for you.

### Discord, Matrix and IRC channels shown in the apps

Each hackerspace's community talks on its own Discord server, Matrix room or IRC channel. Where a community's admins connect a channel, the app shows that channel read-only, so members can follow along without leaving the app. For that we receive and store the messages posted in that channel, with each sender's display name, avatar (where the service has one) and the time they posted. Replies happen on the original service, under its own terms and privacy policy. If you posted in a mirrored channel and want a message removed from the app, delete it there or email us.

### When you use the apps

- **Location.** If you allow it, the app sends your location while you use it, so we can show nearby venues and events and, when you choose to, check you in at a venue. If you also allow location **Always** (in the background), Hot Mess uses it when the app isn't open too: the device tells the app when you arrive at or leave a venue's area (a geofence) or move a significant distance, so it can show you at the venue, to your friends and in its chat, without you opening the app. Some venues use Bluetooth beacons for this. You can change this to "While Using the App" or turn location off in your device settings at any time; the apps still work, but you'll have to pick a city yourself. Hack Different asks only for location while you use it, and uses it only to sort events and spaces by distance; it never uses location in the background.
- **Activity in the app.** Events you RSVP to, venues you check in at, messages you send in venue and city chats, reports and blocks you make, safety pings you send, and calendar feeds you subscribe to.
- **Tribes: labels you choose for yourself.** Hot Mess lets you pick tribes, which are labels for how you identify. Some of them can say something about your sexual orientation, your gender identity or your health, such as HIV status. They are always optional. We store the ones you pick with your account and use them only to show you what you share with other people: someone sees one of your tribes only if they picked the same one, unless an audience has turned on public tribes and you chose to show it. An audience's admins get no exception to that rule. We never use tribes to place sponsored listings or ads, and we never share them with venues, publishers or advertisers. You can remove a tribe at any time, and it is deleted with your account.
- **Finding friends from your contacts.** This is off unless you turn it on. If you do, we text a code to your mobile number to check it is yours, and store the number and the name friends would have saved you under. Your phone then sends us a scrambled code (a hash) for each contact that has a first name, last name and number. We scramble each one again with a secret key before storing it, and we never receive or keep a name or number from your contacts. Two people are suggested to each other only when each has saved the other. Turning it off deletes your number, name and all of those codes.
- **Safety alerts.** If you tap "Get help" at a venue, we store the alert, where in the venue you said you were, anything you wrote, and the messages between you and the venue's staff. The staff on shift at that venue see it with your name, and so does that audience's safety team. Alerts are deleted when your whole account is deleted.
- **Payments.** When you pay a cover charge or coat check, tip a performer, or pay a publisher, the payment goes through Stripe or Square on the account of whoever you're paying. We store what you paid, when, to whom and the payment company's reference, so we can show your pass or receipt and handle refunds. We don't receive or store your card number. A performer sees a tip's amount, and your first name only if you chose to show it; otherwise you appear as Anonymous.
- **What you write for publishers.** If you send an op-ed to a publication, the publication's editors and that audience's admins see it with the name you chose.
- **How you use the app.** Product interaction events, such as the venues, events and screens you view and the features you use, linked to your account, so we can improve the apps and find problems.
- **Support requests.** When you use "Report a problem" or email us, what you write, your email address if you give it, and details about the app and your device (version, model, recent errors) so we can fix it.
- **Device and connection.** Your device model and type, an app-specific device identifier, the app version, and the IP address of each session.

### Public information about venues, events and people

To keep listings accurate, we get events, and the performers and hosts who appear at them, from the organizers who list them, from our ticketing partners Eventbrite and Ticketmaster, and from our own records. This includes names, photos, event times and ticket links. For venues, we read only public details from their Facebook Pages: name, address, opening hours, website and photos. We don't collect posts, comments, reactions or information about the people who follow those Pages.

### ID checks at a venue's door

Some venues check ID at the door with our staff app, Velvet, and some states require them to. The venue's staff scan the barcode on the back of your driver's licence or ID card, or type in an ID that won't scan. This happens whether or not you use our apps. We keep a log for the venue with your name, birth date, age, gender, the ID's expiry date and the time of the check, and the ID number only when it was typed in by hand. The name and ID number are stored encrypted, and we never keep the barcode itself. Each entry is deleted from the log after seven days; our database backups can hold a copy a little longer before they expire. The log isn't linked to any account, and it is used only to check age: never for marketing. The venue can give it to law enforcement when the law requires.

### Event photos

Photographers can share photos from events with the audiences they shoot for. If you appear in a photo and want it taken down, email us and we'll remove it.

## How we use it

We use this information only to run the apps:

- to sign you in and keep your account secure;
- to show you venues, events, people and friends that are relevant to you and near you;
- to let venues and organizers manage their own listings;
- to send safety pings to the people you choose, and safety alerts to the staff of the venue you're at;
- to take payments you choose to make and show your pass or receipt;
- to keep the service working, fix problems and prevent abuse.

We don't sell your information, we don't use it to choose which sponsored listings or ads you see, and we don't share it with advertisers or data brokers. Sponsored listings are placed by city and night. To tell a venue how many people saw its sponsored listing in a week, we count viewers with a code that changes every week and is deleted when the week closes; the venue gets one number, or "fewer than 20".

## Who we share it with

- **Other members of the same audience** see what you choose to make visible in the app, for example your name and picture when you RSVP or check in, and friends who also use the app may see that you're at the same venue.
- **AI providers, for moderation and the AI host.** An audience can turn on automated moderation of its chats, and an AI host that answers in them (in Hot Mess it is called Glam and is always labelled as an AI host). Where it does, the chat messages you send in that audience's venue and city chats are sent, without your name, profile or tribes, to an AI model to check them against the community rules or to write a reply. A message that breaks the rules can be removed, or passed to the audience's moderators, and its sender removed from the chat for up to 24 hours unless a person confirms it; see our [terms](/terms/) for how to appeal. Each audience chooses its provider: Anthropic (Claude) by default, or OpenAI, Google or OpenRouter. The provider processes the messages only to provide this service. We keep the AI's notes on a message for 30 days. Not every audience uses it.
- **Venue staff**, when you pay at their door, send them a message, or send a safety alert there. They see what you sent and the name the app shows for you.
- **Publishers**, when you send them an op-ed.
- **Service providers** that host and run the service for us, under contracts that limit their use of the data to providing that service: Heroku (hosting), Amazon Web Services (cloud infrastructure, including photo storage through Heroku, with our copy of your profile picture, and Kinesis, which streams app activity such as venue views and location updates), Stripe and Square (payments), Eventbrite and Ticketmaster (when an organizer connects their account, we store the OAuth tokens it grants so we can sync their events), Firebase Cloud Messaging and Apple Push Notification service (push notifications), New Relic (performance monitoring), Twilio (the text message with your code, if you turn on finding friends from your contacts), Cloudflare (our websites), Fly.io (the chat server) and Google (Places, for venue details; nothing about you is sent).
- **Google Analytics**, on our websites (audiencekit.com, developer.audiencekit.com and hotmess.social) and in the admin console, to count visits and see which pages people use. It sets first-party cookies and receives your IP address and browser details. We don't link it to your account or use it for advertising. You can opt out with Google's [browser add-on](https://tools.google.com/dlpage/gaoptout).
- **Meta (Facebook)**, when you sign in with Facebook or when we request public Page information, under Meta's own terms and privacy policy.
- **Discord**, when you connect your Discord account or when a community's Discord channel is shown in Hack Different, under Discord's own terms and privacy policy.
- **When the law requires it**, for example to respond to a valid legal request, or to protect someone's safety.

## How long we keep it

We keep your account information for as long as your account exists. Location history is kept only as long as it is useful for check-ins and nearby suggestions. Members see the last 24 hours of a venue or city chat.

When you delete your account in an app, we delete what that app holds about you right away: your membership, the tribes you picked there, RSVPs, safety pings, messages and comments. When you delete your last one, or use [Delete your account everywhere](/data-deletion/), we also delete your profile, profile picture, sign-in tokens, sessions, locations, friend connections, safety alerts, phone number and contact codes, and Discord connection, and revoke the app's access to your Apple ID if you signed in with Apple. If you ask by email instead, we do it within 30 days.

Some things stay after deletion: reports other people made about your messages are kept for the audience's moderators, without the link to your account; records of payments stay with the venue, performer or publisher you paid and with their payment company, which must keep them for tax and accounting; and we keep anything the law requires us to keep, including material we must preserve when we report child sexual abuse material.

## Your choices and rights

- **Change Facebook permissions** at any time in Facebook under Settings & privacy, then Apps and websites. Removing an app there stops it receiving new information from Facebook.
- **Disconnect Discord** at any time in Discord under User Settings, then Authorized Apps.
- **Turn off location** in your device settings.
- **See, correct or delete your information** by emailing [rick@lovewins.media](mailto:rick@lovewins.media). See [Delete your data](/data-deletion/) for the steps.

Depending on where you live, you may have extra rights, such as the right to a copy of your data, to correct it, to delete it, to withdraw a consent you gave, or to object to how we use it. We don't sell personal information or share it for targeted advertising, so there is nothing to opt out of there. Email us and we'll help, wherever you live, and we won't treat you differently for asking. If we say no, you can reply to ask us to look again.

- **Remove a tribe** at any time on your profile.
- **Turn off finding friends from your contacts** in the app's settings, which deletes your number and contact codes.

## Children

The apps are for adults. Many listings are for bars and clubs, and you must be at least 18 to use them, or older if your local law sets a higher age. We don't knowingly collect information from anyone younger. If you think a child has given us information, email us and we'll delete it.

## Security

We protect your information with encryption in transit, access controls and limited staff access. No system is perfectly secure, so please tell us at once if you think your account has been misused.

## Changes to this policy

If we change this policy in a way that matters, we'll update the date at the top and, for significant changes, tell you in the app before the change takes effect.
